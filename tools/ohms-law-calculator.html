What is Ohm's Law?

Ohm's Law is one of the most fundamental principles in electrical engineering. Formulated by German physicist Georg Ohm, it defines the mathematical relationship between Voltage (V), Current (I), and Resistance (R) in an electrical circuit:

  • Voltage ($V$): The electrical potential difference, measured in Volts ($V$).
  • Current ($I$): The flow of electric charge, measured in Amperes ($A$).
  • Resistance ($R$): The opposition to current flow, measured in Ohms ($\Omega$).
  • Power ($P$): The rate of electrical energy consumption, measured in Watts ($W$).

Ohm's Law Formulas

Depending on which values are known, you can use the following equations:

  • Voltage: $V = I \times R$ or $V = \frac{P}{I}$
  • Current: $I = \frac{V}{R}$ or $I = \sqrt{\frac{P}{R}}$
  • Resistance: $R = \frac{V}{I}$ or $R = \frac{V^2}{P}$
  • Power: $P = V \times I$ or $P = I^2 \times R$
