What is Ohm's Law?

Ohm's Law is one of the most fundamental principles in electrical engineering and circuit design. Formulated by German physicist Georg Simon Ohm in 1827, it describes the mathematical relationship between Voltage (V), Current (I), and Resistance (R) in an electrical circuit.

Voltage (V)

The electrical potential difference between two points, measured in Volts (V).

Current (I)

The rate of flow of electric charge through a conductor, measured in Amperes (A).

Resistance (R)

The measure of opposition to the flow of electric current, measured in Ohms (Ω).

Power (P)

The rate at which electrical energy is transferred or consumed, measured in Watts (W).

Ohm's Law Formula Summary

You can express Ohm's Law using the following standard equations depending on your target parameter:

Target Parameter Primary Formula Alternative Formula (with Power)
Voltage (V) V = I × R V = P / I or V = √(P × R)
Current (I) I = V / R I = P / V or I = √(P / R)
Resistance (R) R = V / I R = V2 / P or R = P / I2
Power (P) P = V × I P = I2 × R or P = V2 / R

Practical Example

Suppose you have a 12V DC power supply connected to a heating element with a resistance of 6 Ω:

  • Current Calculation: I = V / R = 12V / 6Ω = 2 Amperes
  • Power Consumption: P = V × I = 12V × 2A = 24 Watts
